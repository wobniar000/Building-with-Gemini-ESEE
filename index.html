Policies

Attendance & Tardy Policy

The class is condensed to 8 weeks; each class is worth 2 traditional class periods. An absence from one class warrants 2 days of missed class. Multiple absences will result in lowering of the course grade. Credit for attendance is only achieved by attending in-person classes on campus; teacher candidates will not be provided the option to Zoom. An unexcused first absence may result in a full letter grade deduction in the final course grade (e.g., from A to B). Missing up to one hour of a class period will be considered a tardy; missing more than one hour will be considered an absence. Two tardies count as one absence.

Late Work Policy

Any assignment not turned in by Tuesday, 11:59 PM (unless noted differently) is considered late. There is a deduction of 10% from the assignment's grade per day. Any assignment over two days late may not receive credit (zero points); after two days, late assignments will be accepted, but may result in zero points. All assignments must be completed and submitted to pass the course. All assignments must be submitted on time to be considered for a letter grade of "A". No Incomplete (I) grades will be given, except for medical reasons with appropriate documentation.

Assignment Submissions & Google Docs

Submit assignments via Lamakū; emailed assignments will not be credited. Name documents: Last Name_First Name_Assignment Title. When using Google Docs, change sharing settings so the instructor can "edit" (University of Hawaii group or Anyone with the link set to Editor). Once the link is submitted to Lamakū, do not revise, edit, or complete the assignment after the timestamp. Work completed on a Google Doc after the timestamp receives a late penalty and violates the academic integrity policy.

Appropriate Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Students are expected to be the primary and predominant authors of all work. Generative AI tools may be used for brainstorming or generating basic ideas, but final submissions must showcase the candidate's own unique thinking, align with course requirements, and be tailored to the school context. Using AI to write or complete assignments on behalf of the student is not permitted. Do not cite external sources generated by AI. Any AI use must be acknowledged with a disclosure statement at the end of the assignment.

Academic Integrity & Ethical Behavior

Cheating, fabrication, tampering, plagiarism, or facilitating such activities will result in failure of the course and possible dismissal from the program. All submitted work must be in candidates' own words. Incidents of suspected academic dishonesty are reported to the Office of Student Conduct.

Electronic Devices in Class

Bring laptops or tablets fully charged. Screens must be kept down until ready to use. Do not engage in personal emails, instant messaging, games, or recreational surfing. Cell phones must be turned off or on silent, unless used as an organizational tool as permitted by the instructor.

STEM Safety

Safety is a priority, a STEM benchmark, and candidate responsibility. Model and teach students safe conduct. Every unit and lesson plan developed must specifically state in writing how it addresses safety. Eating is usually not permitted in the STEM classroom.
